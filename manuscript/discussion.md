# Discussion

## Principal findings

We set out to test whether alveolar epithelial cells in lethal COVID-19 undergo a coherent, directional shift away from the healthy state — what we pre-registered as "epithelial robustness collapse." The data answered this in a more nuanced way than we expected. Of three pre-specified criteria — centroid displacement, within-group dispersion, and condition-associated pseudotime enrichment — two were supported and one was not. COVID-19 alveolar cells are not, on average, further from the healthy centroid in PCA space than healthy cells are from that same centroid (Mann-Whitney U, one-sided, p = 1.0). They are, however, markedly more dispersed (Levene's p < 10⁻³⁰; variance ratio 2.3) and shifted toward higher diffusion pseudotime (permutation p = 0.001; median shift +0.048). The honest summary is that COVID-19 fans alveolar epithelial cells outward into a heterogeneous cloud — a distribution with the same rough center but a broader tail — rather than moving the whole population coherently to a new location. This reframes the robustness-collapse concept: the signal is about *loss of coherence*, not *directional motion*.

## Why centroid displacement failed and dispersion succeeded

The centroid-displacement test was pre-specified because, under a simple "sick cells are further from healthy cells" intuition, it should have been the most direct readout. It was not. The median distance of COVID-19 cells from the healthy centroid (10.68) was actually *lower* than the median distance of healthy cells from their own centroid (11.56); means were similar (12.36 vs. 12.10). Some COVID-19 cells remain near homeostatic identity, while others occupy distant and heterogeneous positions. A uniform-displacement test averages these two populations and sees no signal. A dispersion test captures exactly this structure. We suggest that future studies of epithelial collapse should prefer variance- and trajectory-based metrics over centroid-displacement tests, which implicitly assume a unimodal shift that injury biology does not produce.

## Gene program ordering: what we saw vs. what we expected

Our pre-registered hypothesis was that injury programs would activate in the canonical order interferon → NF-κB inflammatory → repair/differentiation → apoptosis. They did not. AT2 and AT1 identity programs peaked at the lowest pseudotime positions (0.015 and 0.266), apoptosis peaked at 0.591, AT2→AT1 differentiation, NF-κB, and oxidative stress all peaked together at 0.744, interferon response peaked at 0.866, and senescence at 0.935. The dominant structure in the pseudotime ordering is identity loss (AT2 then AT1) in the first third, with all injury-associated programs clustered in the upper half. Within that upper half, the fine-grained ordering is sensitive to gene-list choice: Ablation 8 shows that using MSigDB hallmark sets instead of curated lists inverts the ordering (Spearman ρ = −0.80), and root-choice Ablation 5 shows that rooting pseudotime in a COVID extreme rather than the healthy AT2 centroid also inverts it (ρ = −0.10). We therefore do not claim a specific injury-program cascade. What the data do support is that homeostatic identity declines monotonically with pseudotime and that injury programs rise in the upper half. The finer ordering within the injury phase is a hypothesis-generating observation, not a confirmed sequence.

## Trajectory is alveolar-specific

Ablation 6 added airway epithelial cells (Club, Ciliated, Basal) to the analysis and the condition-associated pseudotime shift collapsed (MPD dropped from +0.048 to near zero). This is biologically coherent — airway and alveolar epithelia have distinct injury responses and including them dilutes the alveolar-injury axis — but it is also a constraint on interpretation. The trajectory we describe is a property of the alveolar compartment considered in isolation. It is not a pan-epithelial lung-injury trajectory. Future work that aims to generalize this framework to "lung epithelial robustness collapse" will need to build compartment-specific trajectories and then compare them, rather than pooling all epithelial cells into a single manifold.

## ECM-high / KRT8⁺ transitional population

The ECM-high population was 3.1-fold enriched in COVID-19 relative to controls (8.5% vs. 2.7%) and occupied intermediate pseudotime positions (mean ≈ 0.3–0.5). Marker expression (KRT8, CLDN4, partial AT1) places these cells in the same molecular family as damage-associated transient progenitors (DATPs) or KRT8⁺ alveolar intermediates described in mouse lung injury (Kobayashi et al., 2020; Strunz et al., 2020) and aberrant basaloid cells in IPF (Adams et al., 2020; Habermann et al., 2020). Their intermediate pseudotime position and elevated frequency in COVID-19 are consistent with accumulation of cells that initiated the AT2→AT1 repair program but have not completed it. This interpretation is correlational — we have a snapshot, not a lineage trace — but it is concordant with the independent mouse-model literature on stalled AT2→AT1 differentiation. We note that the annotation label `ECM-high epithelial` is inherited from the original atlas (Melms et al., 2021); marker expression (Table S3) confirms these cells are the KRT8⁺/CLDN4⁺ transitional population and not an ECM-producing stromal contaminant.

## Robustness of the pseudotime shift

The pseudotime shift itself — as distinct from the program-ordering interpretation — is the most robust finding in this study. Leave-one-donor-out analysis (Ablation 7) preserved the direction of the shift in all 27 iterations, with magnitude ranging from 0.043 to 0.053 (primary value 0.048). No single donor was necessary. The shift also survived the fallback to uncorrected PCA (the Harmony batch correction failed at runtime due to a returned-shape bug in harmonypy; Ablation 3 explicitly tests this fallback). The program-ordering *rank correlation* ρ was 1.0 under curated-only gene programs, 0.88–1.0 under leave-one-donor-out, and dropped sharply only under MSigDB-hallmark substitution and COVID-extreme rooting. The take-home is that the existence and direction of the pseudotime shift is robust; the fine-grained interpretation of *what* that shift means in terms of injury program sequence is sensitive to gene-list choice.

## Relation to prior work on SCP1219

The Columbia/NYP atlas (Melms et al., 2021) has been analyzed previously with emphasis on cell-type compositional changes, immune-epithelial signaling, and identification of pathological macrophage populations. Our analysis is complementary: we focus specifically on the alveolar epithelial compartment and apply trajectory-based rather than cluster-based methods. Our 3.1-fold enrichment of transitional cells in COVID-19 is consistent with the original report's description of aberrant transitional epithelial cells in COVID-19 lungs. We add quantitative pseudotime ordering, explicit ablation-based robustness validation, and an honest accounting of which pre-registered tests did and did not support the robustness-collapse hypothesis.

## Limitations

Several limitations warrant explicit acknowledgment.

**Cross-sectional design.** All cells were collected at a single moment (time of death or organ procurement). Pseudotime is a computational ordering along a manifold, not a measurement of elapsed time. We cannot demonstrate that individual cells traverse the inferred trajectory, nor establish the real-world timescale of any failure process. All dynamic-sounding language is shorthand for "cells exist at these positions on the inferred trajectory."

**Single dataset.** SCP1219 is one cohort (27 donors). Replication in independent lethal-COVID-19 lung datasets (e.g., Delorey et al., 2021; Wendisch et al., 2021) is required before the findings can be considered general. We have not performed this replication.

**Only lethal cases.** The cohort has no mild or moderate COVID-19 cases. We therefore cannot determine whether the pseudotime shift and dispersion increase are specific to lethal disease or are dose-dependent features of SARS-CoV-2 infection in general.

**Post-mortem effects.** Peri-mortem hypoxia and agonal changes may have influenced gene expression patterns at tissue collection, particularly affecting stress-response and apoptosis programs.

**Donor imbalance.** 10 of 27 donors contributed fewer than 300 alveolar cells each, and per-donor counts ranged from 62 to 2,850. We relied on leave-one-donor-out to confirm that no single donor drove the primary finding, but per-donor statistical weight was uneven.

**Gene-program sensitivity.** The specific ordering of injury programs within the upper pseudotime half is not robust to gene-list choice (Ablation 8 MSigDB substitution inverts it). We are confident about the identity-programs-peak-first, injury-programs-peak-second pattern; we are not confident about the fine-grained injury program sequence.

**Known pipeline issues.** (i) Harmony batch correction failed at runtime (harmonypy returned a 1-D array where a 2-D matrix was expected) and the pipeline fell back to uncorrected PCA; Ablation 3 explicitly evaluates this fallback. (ii) The gene GPX1 was absent from the HVG-restricted variance features and was excluded from the oxidative-stress program; other programs may have similar silent dropouts. (iii) Palantir was not installed in the analysis environment, so Ablation 9 (alternative trajectory algorithm) exited with a `palantir_not_installed` flag rather than providing orthogonal evidence. (iv) An earlier version of `common.py` computed displacement effect size from a key that was never populated, resulting in `displacement_effect_size = 0.0` in several Table S6 rows; the fix is documented in Methods.

**Alveolar-specific signal.** As noted above, Ablation 6 shows the pseudotime shift collapses when airway epithelial cells are pooled in. The trajectory applies to the alveolar compartment specifically, not to lung epithelium broadly.

## Future directions

- **Replication.** Re-run this pipeline on independent lethal-COVID-19 lung datasets and on mild/moderate COVID-19 cohorts.
- **Orthogonal trajectory methods.** Install and run Palantir (Ablation 9) and RNA velocity on the same cells to provide direction-independent confirmation of the trajectory.
- **Spatial context.** Apply the same trajectory framework to spatial transcriptomics of COVID-19 lung to test whether pseudotime position corresponds to anatomical organization (distance from infection foci, proximity to immune infiltrates).
- **Compartment-specific trajectories.** Build separate trajectories for alveolar and airway epithelium, then compare — rather than pooling and losing the alveolar-specific signal (Ablation 6).
- **Functional test of transitional cells.** The biggest biologically actionable claim — that KRT8⁺/CLDN4⁺ cells represent stalled AT2→AT1 repair — is testable in lung organoid or ex vivo systems by asking whether these cells can be pushed toward completion of AT1 differentiation.
- **Pre-registered dispersion metric.** Future robustness-collapse studies should adopt a dispersion- or trajectory-based primary metric rather than centroid displacement, which did not detect the effect in our data despite a clear visual and statistical dispersion signal.

## Conclusion

Lethal COVID-19 does not push alveolar epithelial cells coherently away from the healthy state. It fans them out along a continuous pseudotime axis and increases their transcriptomic heterogeneity, with accumulation of a KRT8⁺/CLDN4⁺ transitional population at intermediate trajectory positions consistent with stalled AT2→AT1 repair. Of three pre-registered criteria for "epithelial robustness collapse," two were supported (dispersion, pseudotime enrichment) and one was not (centroid displacement), yielding a moderate composite assessment. The pseudotime shift itself is robust across 27 leave-one-donor-out iterations, alternative root choices, and the batch-correction fallback. The fine-grained ordering of injury programs within the upper pseudotime half is sensitive to gene-list choice and should be treated as hypothesis-generating. The trajectory is a property of the alveolar compartment specifically and dilutes when airway epithelium is included. These results support a reframed model of epithelial robustness collapse as *loss of coherence* rather than *directional displacement*, and highlight dispersion and trajectory-based metrics — rather than centroid displacement — as the appropriate tools for detecting it.
